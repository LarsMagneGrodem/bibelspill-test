Homeschool Advent with the Gospel of Luke

The Journey with Luke is an online Advent calendar game built on the Christmas story in Luke’s Gospel, with a free printable sheet for each day. The child becomes the apprentice of Luke the physician and solves one short case a day, 1–24 December, in 5–15 minutes. Each door follows a Bible passage, most of them in Luke. For ages 8 and up, well into the teens.

How a day can look

  1. Play today’s door. The day’s task in the game usually takes 5–15 minutes.
  2. Read the passage. Look up the reference in your Bible and let a child read aloud.
  3. Do the day’s sheet. Page 1 is an easy start. Page 2 follows today’s door: a text from Luke, a task on the text, today’s clues, and something to talk about and do together.
  4. Talk together. When a door is solved, an adult gets two questions to talk about around the table.

The sheets are free: all 24 days in one PDF, or one day at a time at Activities. The answers and help for adults also has the questions to talk about for each door, week by week. The sheets are A4; choose “fit to page” when you print.

Thinking like a historian

Each day the child checks something Luke tells, and sees how good the historians’ clues are. The stamp says Strong clues, Good clues or Scholars debate. Luke is himself a clue: he writes that he built on eyewitnesses and looked carefully into everything (Luke 1:1–4).

  • The witnesses are tested honestly. Did they see it themselves? Do several say the same? Was it told early? Does it fit with everything else we know?
  • What is invented is kept apart. Many of the people, and the conversations with Luke, are invented for the game, and the game says what is taken from the Bible and from other sources.
  • Classic Christian faith. The Bible is treated as trustworthy, and as God’s word to people.

Questions and answers

Can we use it for homeschool?

Yes. Each door follows a Bible passage, most of them in Luke, and there are free printable sheets for every day, with answers and help for adults.

Can we use the sheets without the game?

Yes. The sheets are free. Page 1 is an easy start that everyone can manage, even those who have not played. If not everyone has played, read “From Luke” on page 2 aloud.

Which Bible passages does it build on?

Mostly Luke 1–2. Some doors go further in Luke, among them chapters 3, 4, 7, 20 and 23–24, and some compare with Matthew 1–2, Acts and 1 Corinthians 15. You can see the passage for each door in the plan above.

Can more than one person use the same device?

Yes, but the game has no profiles, so people who share a device also share the progress. If each player is to have their own journey, they can use their own device or browser.

What if we buy in the middle of December?

Then the doors up to and including today’s date are open at once, and a new door opens each day through to Christmas Eve. You can catch up with the days when it suits you.

Does it work on a phone, a tablet and a computer?

Yes. The game runs in the browser on a phone, a tablet and a computer, with nothing to install. A new door needs the internet the first time it is opened. After that it can be played offline on the same device. On iPhone and iPad, iOS 16.4 or later is needed.
