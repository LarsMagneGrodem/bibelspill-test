The emperor’s decree
Door 7 in the game. Read it yourself in the Bible: Luke 2:1–7.

3 From Luke
The emperor Augustus ordered a census of the whole Roman world. Joseph went from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the town of David, because he was of David’s family. Mary, who was expecting a child, went with him. There she gave birth to her son and laid him in a manger (Luke 2:1–7).
In a census you count. Here the letters have turned into numbers. Use the key to find the emperor and the town. A = 1, B = 2, C = 3 … The emperor: 1-21-7-21-19-20-21-19. The town: 2-5-20-8-12-5-8-5-13.

4 Detective: today’s clues
Today’s check: Luke sets the story in Herod’s time (Luke 1:5). So the registration when Quirinius governed Syria (2:1–2) took place while Herod was still king. The historians say: Scholars debate. Here is why: Josephus tells of a count under Quirinius about ten years after Herod died. But Luke calls his census “the first”. Where we can check Luke, he is often right.
The question is about the year. Luke tells of the journey and the birth clearly. What does a good detective do? Stops looking / Trusts Luke, who looked into things carefully, and looks for more clues / Guesses a year
Extra challenge: Read Luke 2:1–7. Luke mentions five people by name. Find them.

5 Together
Talk together: Luke names Emperor Augustus and Quirinius. Why is it useful when a writer names people who can be checked in other sources?
Do together: Hold a census at home: write down everyone who lives in the house, with their name and the place where they were born.
