3 From Luke
John, the son of Zechariah, preached in the wilderness by the Jordan and baptised people. They asked, “What should we do?” John gave them clear advice: share with those who have nothing. Be honest. Do not use your power to take from others (Luke 3:2–14).
Who was given which advice by John (Luke 3:10–14)? Draw a line from each dot on the left to a dot on the right. 1 The crowd. 2 The tax collectors. 3 The soldiers. 4 Detective: today’s clues
Today’s check: When John began to baptise, Pilate was governor, Herod Antipas ruled Galilee and Lysanias ruled Abilene, and Annas and Caiaphas were high priests (Luke 3:1–2). The historians say: Strong clues. Here is why: The names are found on stones, on coins and in Josephus: a stone with Pilate’s name, a bone box with the name Caiaphas and an inscription about the ruler Lysanias.
Three of the names Luke gives have been found again. Write the name that belongs to each clue. On a stone: … On a bone box: … In an inscription: …
Extra challenge: Read Luke 3:1–2. How many rulers and high priests does Luke mention by name? Count them.

5 Together
Talk together: Luke tells us who was in power when John began to preach, with seven names. Why does a historian do that?
Do together: John said: share with those who have nothing. Find one thing you can share or give away this week.
