The copies
Door 21 in the game. Read it yourself in the Bible: Luke 1:1–4.

3 From Luke
Luke wrote his book by hand. If more people were to read it, someone had to copy it, word for word. We have very many old copies of the New Testament. By comparing them we can be confident about the text. Luke wrote so that Theophilus would know how reliable the things he had been taught were (Luke 1:4).
Try being a copyist yourself. Copy the sentence as carefully as you can, word for word. I have carefully looked into everything from the beginning. (After Luke 1:3). Words in the sentence: Words in your copy: Swap sheets with a friend and check each other’s copy, letter by letter.

4 Detective: today’s clues
Today’s check: The same author wrote the Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles, both to Theophilus (Luke 1:3; Acts 1:1). The historians say: Strong clues. Here is why: Both books are written to Theophilus, and the second points back to the first. The language is alike, and the earliest Christian writers say the same.
Which two books in the Bible are written to Theophilus? Matthew and Mark / The Psalms and Isaiah / Luke’s Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles
Extra challenge: Read Acts 1:1. What was the author’s first book about?

5 Together
Talk together: We have very many old copies of the New Testament. How can comparing them make us confident about what was written?
Do together: Write a short message. Have it copied twice, from copy to copy. Compare the last copy with the original.
