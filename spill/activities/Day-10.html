The guest room and the manger
Door 10 in the game. Read it yourself in the Bible: Luke 2:6–7.

3 From Luke
While they were in Bethlehem, the time came for Mary to have her baby. She gave birth to her firstborn son. She wrapped him in cloths and laid him in a manger, because there was no guest room available for them (Luke 2:6–7). Some Bibles say “the inn”. The word Luke uses can mean a guest room.
Much of what is on Christmas cards is not in the text. What does Luke tell in these two verses? Put one cross on each line. Luke says it / Luke does not say it: Mary wrapped the baby in cloths. An ox and a donkey stood there. The baby was laid in a manger. An innkeeper said no at the door. There was no guest room for them. Draw only what the text says.

4 Detective: today’s clues
Today’s check: Mary wrapped the baby in cloths, as people did with newborns (Luke 2:7). The historians say: Strong clues. Here is why: Wrapping a newborn baby in strips of cloth was usual. The prophet Ezekiel mentions it, and in an old Jewish book the king says that he too was wrapped like that.
Why does it fit well that Luke writes that Mary wrapped the baby in cloths? Because only royal babies were wrapped / Because wrapping newborn babies was usual / Because nobody else did it
Extra challenge: Look up Luke 22:11. Luke uses the same Greek word for the room there as in Luke 2:7. What kind of room does Jesus ask for?

5 Together
Talk together: What does the text really say about the manger and the room, and what have we added ourselves in nativity plays and on Christmas cards?
Do together: Find a Christmas card or a nativity scene. What in the picture is in Luke 2:6–7, and what has someone added?
